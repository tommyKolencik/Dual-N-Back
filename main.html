The practice / A quick guide

How to play.

What does “N” mean?

N is how many cues back you compare. At 2-back, compare cue 3 with cue 1, cue 4 with cue 2, and so on.

  1. Watch the square. Press A when its position matches N cues ago.
  2. Listen to the letter. Press L when the letter matches N cues ago.
  3. Decide separately. Press both if both match, or neither if neither matches.

The first N cues build your memory; no responses are needed. You can mark a match until the next cue arrives.

Feedback as you go

Right means you caught a repeat. Wrong means you pressed when it didn't repeat. After each cue, you'll also see any missed matches and correct passes (no press needed).

Start with 1-back if you're learning. Pause takes a break; Reset abandons the attempt without saving a score.

Behind the project

Motivation

Why I made this

I wanted to improve my memory and discovered Dual N-back. I enjoyed playing it for a while and reached about N = 5. Then I hit a paywall in the app I was using. I tried switching to other apps, but nearly every one I found had a paywall too. I built this website to make the game more accessible.

Reading & research

If you stumbled across this website, here are a few academic articles exploring the potential benefits of Dual N-back. All credit goes to the authors.

A quick note: improvements tend to be strongest on n-back and closely related tasks. Broader benefits for memory or intelligence are less certain. These studies did not evaluate this website.

  1. 2008 · PNASEarly findings

    Improving fluid intelligence with training on working memory (opens in a new tab)

    Susanne M. Jaeggi and colleagues

    This early study reported gains on reasoning tests after dual n-back training. It helped spark interest in the game, though later research has not consistently found the same broader benefits.

  2. 2017 · Journal of Cognitive EnhancementRelated-task gains

    N-back versus Complex Span Working Memory Training (opens in a new tab)

    Kara J. Blacker and colleagues

    Dual n-back training improved performance on a different n-back task. The researchers also observed changes in brain activity, but found no training-specific improvement on their intelligence test.

  3. 2021 · Scientific ReportsMemory-task comparison

    Dual n-back working memory training evinces superior transfer effects compared to the method of loci (opens in a new tab)

    Wenjuan Li and colleagues

    Participants improved at recalling digits and responded faster on a demanding visual memory task. The authors note that similarities between the tests and training may have favored n-back.

  4. 2017 · Psychonomic Bulletin & ReviewThe bigger picture

    Working memory training revisited: A multi-level meta-analysis of n-back training studies (opens in a new tab)

    Anna Soveri and colleagues

    Combining 33 randomized controlled trials of n-back training, this review found moderate improvements on other n-back tasks, but only very small effects on other memory, reasoning, and cognitive-control measures.
